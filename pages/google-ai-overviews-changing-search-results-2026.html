Google AI Overviews Are Changing Search Results in 2026: What Website Owners Need to Know

Google is expanding AI Overviews for some searches, changing how users see websites in 2026. Here’s what the update means for SEO, traffic and publishers.

Staff Writer

Google Search is changing again, and website owners have a new reason to pay attention. For some searches, Google is now dynamically expanding its AI Overview into a much fuller answer instead of showing only a short AI-generated summary.

The change is important for publishers because the expanded AI response can appear before the traditional organic search results, meaning users may need to scroll further before reaching the familiar list of website links.

What is changing in Google Search?

Previously, an AI Overview could appear as a short summary with a “Show more” option. Google is now testing an experience where the AI Overview can automatically expand for certain queries.

The expanded experience can also display an “Ask anything” follow-up box, making the normal Google results page look more similar to Google's AI Mode experience.

Google says the expanded experience is used dynamically when its systems determine that it will be useful for a particular search.

Why does this matter for websites?

The biggest concern for website owners is visibility.

When a large AI-generated answer appears above the traditional results, the first organic website result can be pushed much further down the page. This does not mean that websites have disappeared from Google, but it does change how users may discover them.

For publishers who depend on organic traffic, this makes it increasingly important to create content that provides something more useful than a basic answer.

Will Google AI Overviews reduce website traffic?

It is too early to say that every website will lose traffic because of the change. Google has said that its AI-powered Search features continue to send billions of clicks to websites every week.

However, the position of organic results on the screen matters. If users receive a detailed answer immediately, some searches may require fewer clicks to individual websites.

The impact is likely to vary significantly by topic, search intent and the quality of the websites appearing in the results.

What should bloggers and website owners do?

Website owners should not panic or stop publishing. Instead, this is a good time to improve the quality and usefulness of existing content.

1. Answer the main question quickly

Put the direct answer near the beginning of the article. Don't force readers to go through several paragraphs before finding useful information.

2. Add original information

Content that simply repeats information found on dozens of other websites has less opportunity to stand out. Add original examples, comparisons, screenshots, testing results, explanations or first-hand observations whenever possible.

3. Target specific search questions

Instead of trying to rank for extremely broad keywords such as “AI” or “Google,” create useful articles around specific questions people actually search for.

For example, a smaller website may have a better opportunity with a focused topic such as “how Google AI Overviews affect small websites” than with the extremely competitive keyword “Google AI.”

4. Keep important pages updated

Technology changes quickly. Review older articles and update outdated information, titles, examples and screenshots when necessary.

5. Make your website easy to understand

Use clear headings, short paragraphs, descriptive titles and helpful internal links. A well-organized article is easier for both readers and search engines to understand.

What does this mean for SEO in 2026?

SEO is becoming less about simply reaching the traditional number-one position and more about becoming a useful source that can be discovered across different search experiences.

Google's AI Search developments show that the search results page is continuing to evolve. Website owners should therefore track impressions, clicks, rankings and the queries that bring visitors to their websites.

A page that ranks well today may appear differently tomorrow as Google's search interface changes.

Should you change your SEO strategy?

Yes, but there is no need for a complete SEO reset.

Continue creating original, helpful content while paying closer attention to specific search intent. Build topic authority, connect related articles with internal links and make every page genuinely useful to the reader.

The most important lesson is simple: don't create content only because a keyword has search volume. Create content that gives the searcher a reason to visit your website instead of stopping at a short answer.

Final thoughts

Google's expanding AI Overviews are another major change to the search experience. The new format can place a much larger AI-generated response above traditional website results for some queries.

For website owners, the safest strategy is not to chase every algorithm change. Focus on original information, specific topics, strong user experience and content that provides genuine value.

As Google's AI Search continues to evolve, publishers who adapt their content strategy early may have an advantage over websites that continue using outdated SEO tactics.
