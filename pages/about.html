What we're actually trying to do

Most technology writing optimizes for one of two things: getting published fast, or getting shared widely. Neither of those goals produces writing that's actually useful six months later. Signal optimizes for a much less exciting metric — whether the piece is still correct, and still worth reading, a year after it goes up.

That means we publish less often than most blogs in this space. It also means every author on this site is someone doing the work they write about, not a generalist writer covering a beat they learned last week. Daniel runs infrastructure for a living. Priya trains models for a living. Maya has been editing other people's writing, and her own, for over a decade. Nobody here writes about a tool they haven't used.

How we handle mistakes

We get things wrong sometimes — everyone writing about fast-moving technical topics does. When we do, we correct the piece in place, note the correction and the date at the top of the article, and don't quietly bury the mistake. You can always check the "updated" date at the top of any post to see if it's been revised since it first went up.

What we don't do

  • We don't accept payment for favorable coverage of a product or company
  • We don't publish sponsored posts disguised as regular articles
  • We don't chase every trending topic just because it's trending
